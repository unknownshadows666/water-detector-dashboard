Easy / General Overview

It monitors water levels in real time using an analog sensor connected to a NodeMCU ESP8266. When water reaches a pre-set hazardous threshold, it triggers an immediate local audio alert (buzzer) and sends a remote notification to a smartphone via the Blynk IoT cloud to prevent overflow or water damage.

The NodeMCU features a built-in 2.4GHz Wi-Fi chip (ESP8266) at a very low cost, making it ideal for IoT cloud connectivity without requiring expensive external Wi-Fi shields like an Arduino Uno would.

It uses parallel printed copper traces on a PCB. When water touches the traces, it completes an electrical path between power and ground through the water's natural conductivity, changing the resistance and altering the voltage fed into the analog pin.

Medium / Logic & Calibration

The threshold is set to 620. It was determined empirically by testing the sensor in dry conditions versus submerging it to the target water mark and reading the live ADC output on the Serial Monitor.

Water surface turbulence and electrical noise can cause rapid, erratic signal spikes. Averaging 10 readings over a few milliseconds smooths out transient noise and prevents false alarm triggers.

A time-gating mechanism using millis() ensures mobile push notifications are throttled so they are sent at most once every 30 seconds (30000ms) while the water remains high.

Hard / Architecture & Engineering

Continuous DC current causes rapid galvanic corrosion (electrolysis) on exposed metal traces. In production, power can be supplied to the sensor VCC pin via a GPIO pin—turning power ON only for a few milliseconds during a read cycle and shutting it off—or by switching to a non-contact capacitive water level sensor.

Pin D5 (GPIO 14) is a general-purpose IO pin. Pins labeled S1, S2, SK, SC, etc., are hardwired directly to the onboard flash memory chip that stores firmware instructions; connecting external components to them corrupts instruction fetching and crashes the system.

NodeMCU GPIO pins can only source up to ~12mA at 3.3V DC; high-voltage or high-current AC loads would destroy the microcontroller. To safely control a pump, you must isolate the signal using a Relay Module or an optocoupler circuit.
